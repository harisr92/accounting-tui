Privacy Policy

compter, for iOS, Android and the terminal · Effective 27 October 2026

compter is a bookkeeping app for freelancers: projects, milestones, GST invoices, payments, expenses and tax reports. It is built so that your records stay with you. This policy explains what the app stores, where, and what it never does.

Summary

  • Your records are stored only on your own devices, in a file called compter.redb.
  • If you turn on Google Drive sync, that file is copied to a hidden app folder in your own Google Drive. It is never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Data travels only over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). On your device it sits in the app's private storage, encrypted by your device's own encryption.
  • compter has no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.
  • We, the developers, never receive or see your data.

What the app stores

Whatever you enter, on your device:

  • Your business profile: name, address, GSTIN, invoice terms and logo.
  • Client details: names, addresses, GSTINs and similar details you type in.
  • Projects, milestones, time entries, invoices, credit notes, payments and expenses.
  • App preferences, such as the theme and the sync method you chose.

You put this data in and you control it. Deleting the app (or the compter.redb file on a computer) deletes it from that device.

Google Drive sync (optional)

compter can keep your phone and computer in step through Google Drive. Sync is off until you choose it (in the mobile app's Settings, or with compter drive sign-in on a computer) and sign in with Google.

  • What it accesses: compter asks only for Google Drive's app data permission (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata), plus your email address (openid, email) to show which account is syncing. The app data permission gives compter a hidden folder of its own in your Drive. compter cannot see, open, change or delete any other file in your Drive, and its folder doesn't appear in your Drive's file list.
  • What it does with it: it keeps three files in that folder: the database snapshot (compter.redb), the manifest.json describing it, and a lease.json that says which device has compter open. Your phone and computer, signed in to the same account, read and write those files to hand your records over.
  • Where the data goes: directly between your device and Google Drive, over Google's encrypted connection. It never passes through any server of ours.
  • Your Google account: the app uses your sign-in only to talk to Drive. It never sees or stores your password, and it does not collect your name or contacts. It shows the email address you signed in with in Settings, so you know which account is syncing. The mobile app does not save that address. On a computer it is saved in compter's own settings file, to know which account to use on the next start. It is never sent anywhere except to Google.

compter's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google Drive is used only to provide sync to you, is not transferred to anyone else, is not used for advertising, and is not read by humans.

You can turn sync off at any time, and delete what compter keeps in your Drive: see Deleting your data.

How your data is protected

Your records include sensitive business and financial details. compter protects them like this:

  • On your phone: compter.redb is kept in the app's private storage, which the operating system's sandbox closes to other apps. iOS (Data Protection) and Android (file-based encryption) encrypt that storage at rest while your device is locked with a passcode, PIN or biometrics. compter does not add a separate encryption layer of its own to the file, so please keep a screen lock on your phone.
  • On your computer: the terminal app keeps compter.redb in your user account's application-data folder, protected by your operating system's file permissions. Turn on disk encryption (FileVault, BitLocker or LUKS) to protect it at rest.
  • In transit: everything sent to and from Google goes over HTTPS (TLS): through Google's official libraries in the mobile app, and with certificate checks on a computer. compter makes no unencrypted connections and talks to no server other than Google's.
  • Your Google sign-in: handled by Google's own sign-in page, on your phone or in your computer's browser; compter never sees your password. Access tokens are short-lived (about an hour), are kept in memory only, and are sent only to Google. On a computer, compter also keeps the refresh token Google issues, so you needn't sign in on every start. It is stored in your operating system's keychain (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, the Secret Service on Linux), never in a plain file, and is sent only to Google. compter drive sign-out deletes it. A token Google refuses is cleared at once.
  • Sync settings: on a phone, the only sync detail saved is the sync method, in the app's private preferences. On a computer, compter's settings file records the sync method and the Google account's email address. Neither holds a token.
  • Limited use of Drive: compter reads and writes only its own files in its hidden app folder. Each snapshot is checked against the SHA-256 checksum in its manifest.json before it is used, so a damaged or half-uploaded copy is never loaded.
  • In Google Drive: your synced files are protected by Google's security, including encryption at rest and in transit, and by your Google account's own settings. We recommend turning on 2-Step Verification for your Google account.
  • No servers of ours: there is no copy of your data outside your devices and your own Google Drive, so there is no developer database to be breached, and no one at compter can access your records.
  • Keeping and deleting data: your records are kept until you delete them, as described in Deleting your data.
  • Reporting a security issue: please report it privately through GitHub security advisories.

Deleting your data

Your records exist only on your own devices and, if you sync, in your own Google Drive. We hold no copy, so you delete them yourself, without asking us. Each copy is deleted separately:

  • On your phone: uninstall compter. iOS and Android delete the app's private storage, including compter.redb and the app's preferences.
  • On a computer: delete the compter data folder: ~/.local/share/compter on Linux and macOS (or $XDG_DATA_HOME/compter), %APPDATA%\compter on Windows, or the folder $COMPTER_HOME names. Run compter drive sign-out first to remove the Google sign-in from your keychain.
  • In Google Drive: open drive.google.com, go to Settings → Manage apps, find compter, and choose Options → Delete hidden app data. This removes the synced snapshot, its manifest and the lease. Nothing else in your Drive is touched.
  • compter's access to your Google account: remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions. This stops compter reaching your Drive, but doesn't delete files already there: delete those as above.

Deleting is permanent. Invoices and other records may be ones the law requires you to keep for some years (in India, GST records for six years after the year's annual return is due), so export what you need first.

Sharing and files

When you share or export an invoice PDF or a tax return, the app hands the file to the app you pick (mail, messages, files). What happens next is up to you and that app. When you import a file, compter reads only the file you chose.

What we never do

  • Collect analytics, usage statistics or crash reports.
  • Show ads or use advertising identifiers.
  • Sell, rent or share your data. We never have it to share.
  • Track you across apps or websites.

Children

compter is a business tool and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no data from anyone, children included.
